Science notes & sources — how the performance model works ↗

This educational builder adapts technologies from different NASA missions. They were designed for different spacecraft, so installation compatibility is assumed for the game. Hardware names and indicated published technical specifications come from NASA/mission documentation. When mass, power or other data are unavailable, the calculator uses values clearly marked MODEL ASSUMPTION.

  • Mission assumptions: fixed reference bus = 350 kg; bus operating power = 45 W; launch-mass planning cap = 750 kg; asteroid diameter = 150 m; camera range = 100 km; solar distance = 1 AU; available powered cruise = 90 days. These are hypothetical instructional settings, not the DART mission configuration.
  • Mass: summed bus, selected equipment, and propulsion fuel; impact mass subtracts modeled propellant consumption. The mass radar score compares impact mass to a 750 kg planning scale, while launch feasibility is checked separately.
  • Power Margin: nominal array output at 1 AU minus selected equipment demand in the powered-cruise mode. Lucy solar output is extrapolated from its distant-sun 504 W specification using ideal inverse-square illumination; this does not include degradation, thermal limits or electrical-bus caps.
  • Targeting: geometric target size in pixels = asteroid diameter / (range × camera IFOV). A documented educational index combines image detail and field of view. It does not represent hit probability, optical sensitivity, signal-to-noise ratio or a validated autonomy system.
  • Course Correction: hydrazine baseline based on DART's 55.2 m/s total reported dV99, scaled approximately by spacecraft mass. Electric propulsion uses published reference thrust/specific impulse and an assumed 90-day powered cruise with a simple rocket-equation calculation. Actual transfers, thrust schedules and rendezvous trajectories are outside this model.
  • Data Return: uses each original mission's published link-rate benchmark as an educational index; distances, frequencies, ground station capabilities and transmitter powers differ. The score is not a cross-mission link-budget prediction.
  • Normalization: five axes are a visual teaching tool for comparing designs under one set of assumptions, not a NASA performance certification. An Earth-impact avoidance verdict requires validated intercept and orbital-deflection modeling and is intentionally absent here.
